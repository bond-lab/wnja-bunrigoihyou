Background

The project links each sense of the Japanese WordNet (wnja 2.1) to a record of 分類語彙表 (WLSP), NINJAL's thesaurus of about 100,000 records arranged by meaning. Upstream scripts build and score the candidate pairs; this script only measures how good the scores are, on the part of the table that has hand-made answers.

The two sides of a link

A wnja sense is one word in one synset. Its id carries both: wnja-09876454-n-65694 is the word 兄弟 in synset 09876454-n ("sibling"). The same word in another synset is another sense: 朝 is wnja-15165289-n-82149 ("morning") and also a sense in a synset for a Chinese dynasty.

A WLSP record is one headword in one place in the thesaurus. Its position is a code read left to right from broad to narrow:

1類 (noun) .2140分類項目 兄弟 -01段落 -01小段落 -02語番号

The first four levels name the path 体/主体/家族/兄弟 (the labels column); the 段落 and 小段落 are numbered groups of related words inside that heading. Here is the whole of 小段落 1.2140-01-01, its neighbour, and the start of the next 段落, straight from bunruidb.txt:

The same headword 兄弟 appears twice in one 小段落, once per reading. WLSP does this often (魚 うお/さかな, 毎年 まいねん/まいとし, 風邪 ふうじゃ/かぜ). The gold standard names one of the two; the other is not a wrong link. That one fact drives the strict/lenient split below. Candidates and gold are keyed by 見出し番号 (midashi_no), which is almost always one record; 朝 below is a case where one 見出し番号 (08425) covers two records.

The table the script reads

build/scored.parquet has one row per candidate pair: 168,540 rows, 132 columns. Only three columns matter here, plus the ids:

  • gold_rel, set by candidates.py for senses that are in the dev set and null for the other 165,991 rows. It says how the candidate relates to the gold record of that sense.
  • baseline, the hand-weighted overlap score from score.py (lines 109 to 127): three points per synonym in the gold's 小段落, two per synonym in its 段落, and so on. Values run from 0 to 42.5 and tie often. It is not a probability.
  • p_cv, the scorer's probability for dev rows, from grouped cross-validation so a sense is never scored by a model that trained on it.

Intuition

The script asks two questions of the scores. Ranking: for a sense with several candidates, how high does the right one sit? Thresholding: if we keep every pair with p_cv at or above a cutoff, what share of the kept pairs are right, and what share of the gold pairs did we keep? Each is answered twice, strictly and leniently.

Why lenient: 兄弟

Sense wnja-09876454-n-65694 has exactly two candidates, the two 兄弟 records from the table above. The gold is きょうだい. The scorer cannot tell them apart from context (they share every neighbour) and gives them 0.972 and 0.968:

rankrecordcodegold_relp_cvstrict hitlenient hit
00167801.2140-01-01-02exact0.97211
10167811.2140-01-01-03same_subpara0.96801

Here the ranking is right either way. But at threshold 0.5 both pairs are kept, so strict precision counts one true and one false positive, while lenient precision counts two true. Over the dev set this is why precision at 0.5 is 0.76 strict and 0.88 lenient: 74 kept pairs are a nearby reading or near-duplicate of the gold.

A ranking worked by hand: 朝

Sense wnja-15165289-n-82149 is 朝 "morning". WLSP has the string 朝 in five places; the gold is 08424, the plain 朝 in 時間/朝晩. Sorted by p_cv, descending, with the 0-based rank the script assigns:

The three other rows are the dynasty and place-name senses of 朝 (ちょう), and the scorer buries them. The two morning records are the interesting part:

  • Strict. The only hit is the exact record at rank 1. Top-1 is a miss; the reciprocal rank is 1/(1+1) = 0.5.
  • Lenient. あした（朝） is in the gold's 小段落, so rank 0 is also a hit. The best hit is rank 0: top-1 is a success and the reciprocal rank is 1.
  • Threshold 0.5. Only あした（朝） (0.950) is kept. Lenient precision counts it as a true positive; strict precision counts it as a false positive. The exact record (0.458) is not kept, so this sense is a recall miss in both views: recall only ever counts exact records.

Top-1 accuracy is the share of senses whose best hit has rank 0; MRR is the mean of the reciprocal ranks. The whole dev set, lenient, under p_cv:

So top-1 = 224/305 = 0.734 and MRR = 259.73/305 = 0.852, the two numbers in the laptop-final lenient row. Strict has 207 senses at rank 0 out of 303, giving 0.683 and 0.824.

Why 303 senses strict but 305 lenient? A sense is counted only if it has at least one hit among its candidates. Three of the 306 ambiguous senses have no exact candidate at all, because candidates.py never generated the gold record. Two of them have a same_para candidate (否, 端) and so appear in the lenient count; the third, 掛ける, has only same_item rows and is in neither. Senses that drop out this way do not pull the average down; they vanish from it.

Precision counts hits; recall counts exact

At threshold 0.5, 650 of the 1,361 pairs have p_cv at or above 0.5. Among them 497 are exact and a further 74 are same-小段落 or same-段落; the dev set has 587 exact pairs in all.

viewprecisionrecallF1
strict497 / 650 = 0.765497 / 587 = 0.8470.80
lenient571 / 650 = 0.878497 / 587 = 0.8470.86

Recall is the same in both rows. The script deliberately asks "how many of the gold records did we keep", never "how many of the acceptable records", because a sense with the gold at 0.46 and a near-duplicate at 0.95 (like 朝) has still lost the record the annotator chose.

Decoys

Every dev sense was linked to something, so the real candidates give the scorer no example of a word with no right answer. candidates.py adds two decoys per dev sense: records of other words in the same 類 but a different 分類項目. 丁度 gets もので (他//接続/展開) and よもや (他//判断/判断); 付ける gets 破ける (用/関係/作用/破壊). The last line of the block reports what share of the 1,188 decoys scored below 0.5: 1,187 did (median p_cv 0.007), so 0.999. The one survivor is 丁度 → もので at 0.609.

Code

One entry point and four small functions. In the order they run:

1 · What counts as a hit

evaluate.py:32 and evaluate.py:35–46 
LENIENT = ("exact", "same_subpara", "same_para")

def with_hit(df: pl.DataFrame, lenient: bool) -> pl.DataFrame:
    ok = LENIENT if lenient else ("exact",)
    return df.with_columns(pl.col("gold_rel").is_in(ok).cast(pl.Int8).alias("hit"))

The whole strict/lenient distinction is one tuple. same_item is not in it, so 掛ける's two candidates in 2.1513-14-08 are misses in both views. For 兄弟, strict gives hit = [1, 0] and lenient [1, 1].

2 · Ranking over ambiguous senses

evaluate.py:49–76 
ranked = (
    df.sort([pl.col("sense_id"), pl.col(score)], descending=[False, True])
    .with_columns(
        pl.int_range(pl.len()).over("sense_id").alias("rank"),
        pl.len().over("sense_id").alias("n"),
    )
    .filter((pl.col("n") > 1) & (pl.col("hit") == 1))
    .group_by("sense_id")
    .agg(pl.col("rank").min())
)

Sort by sense, then by score descending; number the rows within each sense from 0; note how many candidates the sense has; keep only hits of senses with more than one candidate; take the best (lowest) rank per sense. For 朝 this yields rank 1 strict and rank 0 lenient. The 290 lone-candidate senses fail n > 1 and are gone, and so is any sense with no hit, which is the 303-versus-305 effect. Then:

rank = ranked["rank"]
return {
    "senses": len(ranked),
    "top1": float((rank == 0).mean()),
    "mrr": float((1.0 / (rank + 1)).mean()),
}

Because rank is 0-based, rank == 0 is top-1 and 1 / (rank + 1) is the reciprocal rank: 1 for 兄弟, 0.5 for 朝 strict. An empty ranked returns zeros rather than dividing by nothing (evaluate.py:69–70).

3 · Precision, recall, F1 at a threshold

evaluate.py:79–100 
kept = df.filter(pl.col(score) >= threshold)
tp = int((kept["hit"] == 1).sum())
exact_kept = int((kept["gold_rel"] == "exact").sum())
n_gold = int((df["gold_rel"] == "exact").sum())
precision = tp / len(kept) if len(kept) else 0.0
recall = exact_kept / n_gold if n_gold else 0.0

tp depends on the hit column, so it changes with the view; exact_kept and n_gold test gold_rel directly and do not. At 0.5: len(kept) = 650, tp = 497 strict or 571 lenient, exact_kept = 497, n_gold = 587. Lone-candidate senses are not filtered out here: every one of the 1,361 pairs is in df, so a lone candidate scored above the threshold counts toward precision and recall even though it never entered the ranking.

The baseline row gets empty P/R/F1 cells: its values (0 to 42.5) are overlap counts, so a probability cutoff of 0.5 would keep almost everything. Its ranking columns are real, though, and are what the first row of every block shows.

6 · The decoy line

if len(decoys):

The cutoff is args.thresholds[1], the second threshold, which is 0.5 by default. It is a position, not a value: --thresholds 0.4 0.6 would test the decoys at 0.6, and a single --thresholds 0.5 would raise IndexError here when decoys are present. A table built before decoys existed has len(decoys) == 0 and simply gets no line.

Glossary

TermWhat it means here
wnja senseone word in one synset of the Japanese WordNet, e.g. wnja-09876454-n-65694 = 兄弟 in the sibling synset.
WLSP record / 見出し番号one headword at one position in 分類語彙表; candidates and gold are keyed by the 見出し番号, which usually names one record.
段落, 小段落numbered groups of related words inside a 分類項目; the second and third numbers of a code such as 1.2140-01-01-02.
dev setthe 596 wnja senses with a gold WLSP record, derived from the TUFS basic vocabulary by build_devset.py.
ambiguous sensea dev sense with two or more candidates (306 of 596); the only senses ranking is computed on.
strict / lenienta hit is the gold record only, or also any record in the gold's 小段落 or 段落 (LENIENT, evaluate.py:32).
top-1, MRRshare of senses whose best hit is ranked first; mean of 1/(rank+1) over senses. Both over senses that have a hit.
p_cvcross-validated probability from score.py: each dev sense is scored by a model that did not train on it.
decoya record of a different word, added as a known negative; two per dev sense, 1,188 in all.

Background

The project links two Japanese sense inventories. A wnja sense is one word in one synset of the Japanese WordNet 2.1, such as wnja-02018049-v-10632: 乗る in the synset 乗船・搭乗・乗る・乗り込む・乗車 … 「（電車、バス、船、航空機など）に乗り込む」, ILI i31829. A WLSP record is one line of the 分類語彙表 (Word List by Semantic Principles), identified by a record ID and a code such as 2.1541-02-01-01, read as 類.分類項目-段落-小段落-語番号: class 2 (用, verbs), item 1541 乗り降り・浮き沈み "boarding and alighting, floating and sinking", paragraph 02, sub-paragraph 01, word 01. The labels column spells out the path: 用/関係/作用/乗り降り・浮き沈み.

The headword 乗る appears in several WLSP records, and so do its written variants. candidates.py pairs each wnja sense with every WLSP record whose headword matches the word's lemma, a form, a する stem or (for kana words) the reading, and writes one row per pair to build/candidates.parquet. For 乗る that is five real candidates:

The features the table already carries

Every numeric column that is not an identifier or a label becomes a feature (feature_columns). Those present in this build:

  • How the strings matched. lex is a string column (lemma 114,567 rows, yomi 43,357, form 9,836, suru 780) and pos_ok says whether the wnja POS agrees with the WLSP class. 浴する matched the noun record 浴 by suru; 丁度 matched ちょうど and the furniture word 調度 by yomi.
  • Structural overlap. Sixteen ov_* counts: how many of the synset's members (syn), hypernyms (hyper), hyponyms (hypo), co-hyponyms (sib), similar (sim) and antonym (ant) synsets have a word in the candidate's 小段落 (subpara), 段落 (para), 分類項目 (item) or 中項目 (middle). For the boarding record of 乗る: ov_syn_para=3, ov_syn_item=3, ov_hyper_item=1, ov_hyper_middle=4, ov_hypo_middle=1, ov_ant_item=2. Beside them the sizes of the neighbourhoods (n_syn=10, n_hyper=14 …) and of the WLSP groups (size_subpara=2, size_para=27, size_item=132, size_middle=5328), plus yomi_match, has_yomi and go_no.
  • Tier columns. features_chive.py added nine chive_* word-vector cosines (null for 11,957 to 25,748 rows where a word has no vector; chive_has marks the 3,299 rows with none) and features_sent.py added sent_multilingual_e5_small_cat, _sub and _ctx, the cosines between the synset text and the record's category path, headword neighbours and wider context.
  • Priors. n_cands, n_senses and sense_rank describe the word, not the pair; they are excluded unless --priors is given (why).

The labels

The dev set (dev/devset.tsv, 734 rows from the TUFS basic vocabulary) gives a gold WLSP record for 596 wnja senses. candidates.py turns that into two columns. gold_rel says how a candidate relates to the gold record, and gold is the training label derived from it:

gold_relrowsgoldexample (sense 乗る or 魚)

other6730乗る → 067149 因果, 086567 脅迫・中傷・愚弄など

So 2,448 rows are labelled (587 positive, 1,861 negative) and 101 dev rows in the gold's own group are left unlabelled: WLSP lists the same headword more than once nearby, and calling those negatives would teach the model to reject genuine many-to-many links. The decoys exist because 290 of the 596 dev senses have a single candidate, which is the gold by construction; without decoys they would carry no negative at all. The decoy/other/same_* classes are made in candidates.py; this script only reads gold and gold_rel.

Intuition

Each pair is scored on its own: a row of numbers in, a probability out. The script never asks "which candidate is best for this sense"; it asks "is this pair a link". That is what lets a sense keep two links (4,266 senses do) and lets a sense with one candidate still get a low score.

The rel_* trick

Look at the e5 ctx column above: 0.870 to 0.927. Over the whole table the 5th to 95th percentile of sent_multilingual_e5_small_cat is 0.812 to 0.888, and a decoy about deceit scores 0.873. Sentence-embedding cosines sit in a narrow band, so a tree asked "is the cosine above 0.88?" learns little. What separates the candidates of one sense is their order, so add_derived subtracts the best value of the same sense: rel_x = x − max(x) over sense_id. The best candidate gets 0 and the rest go negative. The same seven rows on the ctx cosine, to scale:

The large green dot is the gold record; the whole spread is 0.057 on a 0 to 1 axis. After subtraction it is 0 for the gold and −0.022 to −0.057 for the rest, which a depth-2 tree splits easily. In the fitted model the raw sent_multilingual_e5_small_cat has importance 0.002 and its rel_ twin 0.072; the top feature overall is rel_sent_multilingual_e5_small_ctx at 0.35 (top_weights). chiVe cosines spread more (5th to 95th percentile of chive_syn_item: 0.30 to 0.84), but their rel_ versions also lead their raw ones.

Two edges to know about

Many-to-many is allowed. No feature says how many candidates a word has, so two records can both pass. 可能 (wnja-01821266-a-0) is linked to both 1.1346-01-02-01 (noun class, 体/関係/様相/難易・安危, p 0.616) and 3.1346-01-01-01 (adjective class, p 0.55). In all, 4,266 senses have more than one link; 32,993 of the 92,836 links come from senses with several candidates.

Lone candidates are mostly unverified. 59,848 senses have exactly one real candidate, and 59,843 of those pairs score at or above 0.5 (median 0.934). Only five fall below, such as 成る → 成る 2.1220 成立 at 0.281 and お腹 → 御中 (a letter-writing term, form match, POS mismatch) at 0.04. The dev set has no single-candidate sense whose only candidate is wrong (every dev word was linked), so the model has never seen that case. The decoys show it can reject unrelated records (1 of 1,188 decoys passes: 丁度 → もので, a yomi match at 0.572), but a lone candidate that is the same word in a different sense is harder and unmeasured. That is why n_cands is kept in the output: filter on it, or sample lone links with judge_claude.py --single-sample.

And the gold is not always right. 魚 in wnja-07775375-n 「食料として用いられる、魚の肉」 (fish as food) has four candidates. The gold is 063577 in 1.5504 魚類 (the animal), because the TUFS concept 魚 links to both the animal and the food synset. The model prefers the food record 051220 1.4323 魚・肉 (p 0.49) over the gold (0.48), and the only link written is 063576, the neighbouring 魚 entry in the gold's 小段落 (0.69), which the lenient evaluation counts as correct. Reading dev disagreements by hand is part of the job.

1 · Which columns are not features

score.py:38–56

Three sets drive everything downstream: the identifier columns that go into the output TSV, the columns that must never be features (the IDs plus labels and the model's own outputs), and the priors that are features only on request.

NON_FEATURES = set(ID_COLUMNS) | {"lex", "gold", "gold_rel", "p", "p_cv", "baseline", "evidence"}
# Priors about how many candidates or senses a word has.  They help top-1
# accuracy but penalise genuine many-to-many links, so they are off by default.
PRIORS = {"n_cands", "n_senses", "sense_rank", "inv_cands", "inv_senses"}
LEX_TYPES = ("lemma", "form", "suru", "yomi")
POS_PAIRS = tuple(f"{a}{b}" for a in "nvar" for b in "nvar")

POS_PAIRS is the sixteen strings nn, nv, na, nr, vn, … rr. The table has 321 rows whose wnja POS is x (ふん and other interjections); they match none of the sixteen and get all-zero one-hots.

2 · Derived features

score.py:59–88

One with_columns call adds every derived column. The first block is fixed: a one-hot per match type, a one-hot per POS pair, and the two inverse priors. The loop then walks the existing columns and derives from each by prefix.

for c in df.columns:
    if c.startswith("ov_"):
        rel = c.split("_", 2)[1]
        cols.append((pl.col(c) / pl.max_horizontal(pl.col(f"n_{rel}"), 1)).alias(f"fr_{c[3:]}"))
        cols.append((pl.col(c) > 0).cast(pl.Int8).alias(f"any_{c[3:]}"))
    elif c.startswith("size_"):
        cols.append(pl.col(c).log1p().alias(f"log_{c}"))
    elif c.startswith(("chive_", "sent_")) and c != "chive_has":
        cols.append((pl.col(c) - pl.col(c).max().over("sense_id")).alias(f"rel_{c}"))

For ov_syn_para, rel is syn, so the fraction divides by n_syn (floored at 1 so an empty neighbourhood does not divide by zero). What the gold row of 乗る gains, with the inputs it came from:

lex="lemma"                     -> lex_lemma=1  lex_form=0  lex_suru=0  lex_yomi=0
pos="v", pos_wlsp="v"           -> pp_vv=1, the other fifteen pp_* = 0
n_cands=5, n_senses=11          -> inv_cands=0.2  inv_senses=0.091   (priors: not used)
ov_syn_para=3,  n_syn=10        -> fr_syn_para=0.3    any_syn_para=1
ov_hyper_item=1, n_hyper=14     -> fr_hyper_item=0.071 any_hyper_item=1
ov_ant_item=2,  n_ant=2         -> fr_ant_item=1.0    any_ant_item=1
size_middle=5328                -> log_size_middle=8.581   (= ln 5329)
sent_..._ctx=0.927, sense max 0.927 -> rel_sent_multilingual_e5_small_ctx=0.000
chive_para_max=0.372 on the 因果 row, sense max 1.0 -> rel_chive_para_max=-0.628

The rel_ branch deliberately skips chive_has, a 0/1 flag, and the .over("sense_id") window makes the subtraction per sense without a group-by and join. Null cosines (rows with no chiVe vector) stay null here and are filled with 0 at training time (fit_and_predict).

Any numeric column not excluded is a feature, in table order, which is what lets later scripts add chive_*, sent_* or judge_* columns without touching this file. In this build that is 109 features: 30 structural, 9 chiVe, 3 e5, 4 lex_, 16 pp_, 16 fr_, 16 any_, 4 log_ and 11 rel_; --priors would make it 114. The priors are off because a count of candidates says "this word is ambiguous", which pushes every pair of an ambiguous word down and every lone pair up, regardless of the pair itself.

150 trees of depth 2 (each tree asks at most two questions), a small learning rate and 80% row subsampling: a deliberately shallow model for 2,448 training rows. It fits in about two seconds. random_state=0 makes a refit reproduce p to the third decimal, which is how the numbers on this page were checked. The lr alternative scales the features first and balances the 587 : 1,861 class ratio; the README's results table says gbt is better on dev.

7 · Cross-validate, refit, predict

score.py:174–214

The heart of the script. Four arrays are built once: the feature matrix for every row (nulls to 0), a mask of labelled rows, their labels, and their sense IDs as groups.

x_all = df.select(features).fill_null(0.0).to_numpy().astype(float)
labelled = df["gold"].is_not_null().to_numpy()
if not train_single:
    labelled &= (df["n_cands"] > 1).to_numpy()
x = x_all[labelled]
y = df["gold"].fill_null(-1).to_numpy()[labelled]
sense_ids = df["sense_id"].to_numpy()
groups = sense_ids[labelled]
in_dev = df["gold_rel"].is_not_null().to_numpy() if "gold_rel" in df.columns else labelled

Then five folds, grouped by sense: a sense's rows are either all in the training part or all held out, so the model scoring 乗る has never seen any row of 乗る. Each held-out model predicts every dev row of its senses, including the 101 unlabelled same_* rows, so evaluate.py can rank them too.

p_cv = np.full(len(df), np.nan)
for train, test in GroupKFold(n_splits=folds).split(x, y, groups):
    model = make_model(kind).fit(x[train], y[train])
    held = in_dev & np.isin(sense_ids, np.unique(groups[test]))
    p_cv[held] = model.predict_proba(x_all[held])[:, 1]
model = make_model(kind).fit(x, y)
p = model.predict_proba(x_all)[:, 1]

On this build each fold trains on 1,958 or 1,959 rows and holds out 118 to 120 senses (489 or 490 labelled rows). p_cv is set for the 2,549 dev rows and stays NaN for the other 165,991; the final model, fit on all 2,448 labelled rows, writes p for all 168,540.
